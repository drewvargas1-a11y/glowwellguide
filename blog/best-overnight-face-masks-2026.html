Frequently Asked Questions

Overnight masks are typically formulated in a thicker, more occlusive gel or cream texture meant to sit on skin longer and deeper than a standard moisturizer, often including additional actives for repair, brightening, or exfoliation.
It depends on the formula — hydrating, gentle options like aloe-based masks can often be used nightly, while ones with stronger acids or vitamin C are usually better limited to 2-3 nights a week to avoid over-exfoliating.
Quality overnight masks are formulated to absorb without heavy residue transfer, but sleeping on a clean pillowcase and giving the mask a few minutes to set before bed reduces any risk regardless of formula.
