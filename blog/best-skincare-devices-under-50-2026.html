Frequently Asked Questions

Budget skincare devices can deliver real results, but expectations should be calibrated. Ice rollers genuinely reduce puffiness — cold vasoconstriction works regardless of price. Sonic cleansing brushes in the $25–$50 range use the same oscillating-motor technology as $150 Foreo models. Budget RF devices deliver real radiofrequency energy but at lower intensities — results are slower and more subtle. Bottom line: for non-energy tools (ice rollers, cleansing brushes), budget versions work well. For energy devices (RF, microcurrent), the gap between budget and premium is significant.
For most beginners, start with a sonic facial cleansing device (like the PMD Clean) or a gua sha/ice roller set. Both are low-risk and deliver noticeable results quickly. Cleansing devices improve makeup removal and product absorption in days. Ice rollers reduce puffiness immediately. Save energy devices (RF, LED panels, microcurrent) for after you've established a solid skincare routine.
Most sonic cleansing brushes should be used once daily, maximum — over-cleansing strips the skin's natural moisture barrier, especially when combined with chemical exfoliants (AHA, BHA, retinol). For sensitive skin, 3–4 times per week is sufficient. Avoid using on active breakouts or broken skin. Replace bristle heads every 3 months; silicone heads last 6+ months.
