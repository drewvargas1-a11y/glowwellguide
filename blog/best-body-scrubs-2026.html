Body scrubs work by physically removing dead skin cells to reveal smoother, brighter skin underneath — and the best ones add moisturizing oils that leave skin softer than before treatment. But not all scrubs are created equal: coarser particles like walnut shell or apricot pits can cause microtears, while sugar and coffee grounds are gentler and dissolve with use. Chemical exfoliants (BHA/AHA) skip physical abrasion entirely and work within the pore, making them the best choice for specific conditions like keratosis pilaris.

We evaluated each scrub on exfoliation effectiveness, ingredient quality, post-use skin feel, fragrance, and how long the product lasts per use — because a $30 scrub you use sparingly can be more economical than a $10 one you go through in two weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Physical scrubs should be used 2–3 times per week for most skin types. Over-exfoliating causes irritation, microtears, and damaged skin barrier — signs include redness, tightness, and increased sensitivity. Dry or sensitive skin does best with 1–2 times per week. Chemical exfoliants like BHA/AHA body treatments can be used daily on problem areas.
Sugar granules dissolve as you scrub, providing progressively gentler exfoliation — making them excellent for sensitive skin and larger body areas. Salt (sea salt, Himalayan) has harder, more angular particles for more intense exfoliation. Salt also contains trace minerals with benefits for circulation. Salt scrubs are better for very rough areas like feet and elbows; sugar scrubs are better for the full body and sensitive skin.
Yes — always moisturize immediately after exfoliating while your skin is still slightly damp. Exfoliation temporarily makes skin more permeable, so moisturizer absorbs more effectively post-scrub. Many scrubs contain oils that leave a residue — pat dry gently to preserve the oil layer, then apply body lotion on top for best hydration results.
