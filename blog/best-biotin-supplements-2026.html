Biotin (vitamin B7) is the hair supplement the beauty industry has promoted for decades — but the science is more nuanced than the marketing suggests. Biotin is essential for keratin synthesis (the protein that makes up hair, skin, and nails), but true biotin deficiency is relatively uncommon. The supplements most likely to produce real results are those that address multiple root causes of hair thinning — stress hormones, DHT sensitivity, nutritional gaps — not just biotin alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Biotin helps with hair growth primarily in people who have a deficiency — which is more common than previously thought, especially in people who consume raw eggs, have inflammatory bowel disease, or are pregnant. For people with adequate biotin levels, supplementation shows minimal benefit for hair growth specifically. However, biotin is safe, inexpensive, and supports skin and nail health even if hair response is modest.
Hair grows approximately 0.5 inches per month regardless of supplementation. Biotin doesn't speed the growth cycle — it supports hair structure and strength. Most people notice stronger, thicker hair with less breakage at 2–3 months. Visible length changes attributable to biotin (via reduced breakage) typically appear at 4–6 months of consistent daily use.
Hair growth studies use 2,500–5,000mcg. Anything above 10,000mcg doesn't provide additional hair benefits. High-dose biotin (above 5,000mcg) can interfere with certain lab tests — thyroid panels and troponin (heart) markers specifically. Always tell your doctor you're supplementing before bloodwork, and stop supplementing 3–5 days before tests if possible.
