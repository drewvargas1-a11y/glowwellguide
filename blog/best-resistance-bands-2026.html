Frequently Asked Questions

Can resistance bands build real muscle, or are they only for rehab?

Resistance bands absolutely build real muscle — multiple randomized controlled trials show band training produces comparable gains in muscle strength and hypertrophy to free weights when progressive overload is applied. The elastic force curve (resistance increases through range of motion) actually provides advantages in specific exercises. Bands are not inferior to weights — they're a different tool with different mechanical properties that produce comparable outcomes for most strength training goals.

What's the difference between loop bands and flat resistance bands?

Loop bands (mini bands, 9–12 inches) are for lower body activation, glute exercises, and mobility. Long flat bands (therapy bands, 4–6 feet) are for upper body, PT, stretching, and pulling motions. Pull-up assist bands (41-inch heavy loops) are for pull-up assistance and heavy compound movements. A complete setup ideally includes all three types for different movement patterns.

How do resistance bands compare to weights for injury recovery?

Bands are frequently the preferred choice for rehabilitation — hence TheraBand's presence in virtually every PT clinic. Key advantages: variable resistance matches tissue tolerance better than fixed loads; elastic force produces lower joint shear forces; bands allow precise eccentric loading critical for tendon repair; and they can replicate specific movement patterns with load control that free weights can't. For active rehabilitation, bands typically produce better clinical outcomes with lower re-injury risk.
