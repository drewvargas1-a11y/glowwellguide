There are two broad types of lash serum. Some contain prostaglandin analogs (such as isopropyl cloprostenate), which tend to show the most dramatic results but can cause irritation and, rarely, darkening of the eyelid or iris. Others rely on peptides, oils, and botanicals, which are gentler but usually more subtle.

Results take weeks, not days. Most brands suggest about 4–6 weeks for visible change and around 3 months for the full effect. Patch test, stop if irritation occurs, and talk to an eye doctor if you have eye conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Prostaglandin-analog serums are the most likely to produce visible length and fullness. Peptide and oil formulas condition lashes and may help with breakage but are usually more subtle.
Most people use them without problems, but prostaglandin-based serums can cause redness, itching, or eyelid darkening. Patch test and stop if you have a reaction.
Results fade gradually over weeks to months after you stop, as lashes go through their natural growth cycle.
Many prostaglandin-free serums are marketed as extension-safe; oil-based products can loosen extension glue.
