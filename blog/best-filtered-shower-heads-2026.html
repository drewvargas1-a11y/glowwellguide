— For Skin, Hair & Water Quality

For anyone with sensitive skin, eczema, psoriasis, or color-treated hair, a filtered shower head can produce genuine improvements in skin hydration and hair moisture over weeks of consistent use. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a filtered shower head actually help skin and hair?

The evidence is meaningful but modest. Chlorine strips natural skin oils and is a known irritant. Several dermatology studies link hard water to increased eczema severity. Benefits are most pronounced for people with sensitive skin, eczema, psoriasis, or color-treated hair — and in areas with heavily chlorinated or hard water. The changes are typically subtle over weeks of use, not instant transformations, but consistent and appreciated by most users who start.

How often do filtered shower head filters need replacing?

Most filters are rated for 6,000–10,000 gallons or 6–12 months typical use. At 8 minutes per shower at 2 gallons per minute, that's approximately one replacement per 3–6 months for a single person. Households with heavily chlorinated or hard water exhaust filters faster. Neglecting replacement is counterproductive — expired filters provide no chlorine removal and can harbor bacteria growth.

What's the difference between a shower filter and a water softener?

A shower filter primarily removes chemical contaminants (chlorine, chloramines, heavy metals) but does NOT meaningfully soften water. A water softener specifically addresses hardness (calcium/magnesium) via ion exchange. For dry skin from hard water specifically, a whole-home softener delivers more comprehensive results. For chlorine sensitivity, a shower filter is highly effective. Many households benefit from both.
