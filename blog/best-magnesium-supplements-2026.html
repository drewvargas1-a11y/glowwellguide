Magnesium is involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions in the body — including protein synthesis, muscle and nerve function, blood pressure regulation, and melatonin production. Roughly 48% of Americans consume less than the daily recommended amount, and blood tests often miss deficiency since the body maintains blood magnesium levels at the expense of tissue stores.

Not all magnesium supplements are equivalent. The form matters enormously: magnesium oxide has only 4% bioavailability; magnesium glycinate absorbs at 40%+. We evaluated four products on form, bioavailability, dose, manufacturing quality, and how well users report sleep, stress, and recovery improvements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Magnesium glycinate is the best form for sleep. It's bound to glycine — an amino acid that crosses the blood-brain barrier and promotes sleep onset independently. Magnesium glycinate is also the most bioavailable and gentlest on the stomach. Take 200–400mg elemental magnesium glycinate 30–60 minutes before bed. Avoid magnesium citrate for sleep — it's absorbed quickly and can have a laxative effect at higher doses.
Most people notice improved sleep quality within 1–2 weeks of consistent supplementation. Correcting a true deficiency takes 4–6 weeks for full effect. Magnesium doesn't act as a sedative — it doesn't make you drowsy immediately. Instead, it supports the nervous system's ability to shift into parasympathetic (rest) mode, improving sleep quality over time.
The tolerable upper intake is 350mg/day of elemental magnesium from supplements (dietary sources don't count toward this). Exceeding this commonly causes diarrhea and GI distress. True magnesium toxicity from supplements alone is rare in people with healthy kidneys. If you have kidney disease, consult a doctor before supplementing — impaired kidneys can't excrete excess magnesium effectively, which can cause serious complications.
